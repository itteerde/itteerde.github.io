Reflections

By the lost Cleric Enona

“Aetherial Expanse?”

What is the “Aetherial Expanse?” I have been told is was either a god, having sacrificed themselves in ancient times, or a catastrophe, caused by ancient mortals, rather than nature, or the super-natural. I don't know. It rings wrong to me. Though, who am I to judge a world I know nothing about? However things might be here, I will have to figure it out, at last as far as it concerns me -- which given my predicament seems to be a lot of it all.

Who am I here? Who was I before? How did I get here? It is well known to the peoples of this realm, that about a century ago, two empires from different, but certainly mortal, realms discovered the Expanse. A century, about five generations, means that one cannot necessarily trust the story tellers' account of that history. Well, may that be true accounts or the history written by victors, the Kingdom of Ayris, and the Empire of Karel are what en large controlls the fate of the peoples here -- though not only Freeborne take liberties with their rule of law.

The historians here seem to agree on both of them having had their world changed revolutionary by their discoveries here -- that it is “aetherium” that allowed them to raise quickly to powers they could not have imagined where they came from.

Pirates

The natives, if one can call them that, when scholars claim that everything and -one flesh-and-blood are invasive species from the mortal realms, call the struggle between the explorers, collonizers -- “War of the Invaders”. And given the multitude of communities and crews having spawned from that war's defectors, and the stories of their iron rule, who would I be to jude those who reject it, and try to make their own future, on that fact alone, that is. There are certainly those who slaughter and plunder. Still, it seems freedom lies with those outlaws, and as long as there is a code, I guess I'll live with that better than being pressed into either warrying empires' service.

Loyality to the Karelagne

Why would anyone having been press-ganged into service would feel any loyality to the emperor, or the church that keeps declaring the justification of the mission of the barbarians, while both get rich, now not only on gold and silver, but aetherium, too? And I do not think I would have survived my first year under that damn captain and his sadist first mate!

And so, marked for 20 years forced labor on the Crown's vessels, now having defacted, there never can be a return home. Therefore who is to judge me on taking to the rum and looking for whatever joy, or what one might mistake for it for a while? Damn the hypocritical theologicans and inquisitors. Damn the lords all to happily convict those whom they point out to them -- as that makes them free labor. The clergy would claim there are no Karelagne slaves. Well, most of their sailors will see that differently.

Fortunately it has been established that everyone here is hot. So I'll keep finding hot girls to drink and roll with and if the piracy allows to fuck the Karelagne navy over every now and then all the better.

A new Hope

And I put no value in what that inquisitor of Karelagne said about my lose mouth blasphemous thoughts. I didn't change what I think about those things at all, but when that monstrous pair of a captain and first mate were on the certain path to my destruction I was heard. And Unnammon did not care that I used the wrong words, confusing the visions with what I was taught at home first. She gave me Healing Hands to survive the torment, and wings to take to her jet stream and soar high and fast and get to that sheltering island allmost with ease.

I do not know if she has plans for me, or if she is just the rare loving and benevolent Goddess, but she seems not to have any problems with who I am. I trust she'll let me know if I owe her a service. It seems the peoples of the Expanse, who have known her much longer than I don't think she is calling for some fossilized organized religion or church. Maybe she is indeed the Goddess of Freeborn, too, rather than calling for submission and acceptance of serving and suffering, like the clerics at home thaught the people the gods demanded from them.

The Empire Strikes Back

Expecatations rarely match what reality brings. I would have thought that settling down on an island, working at the harbor, sometimes some work to be found at the local community, not unlike the trying to make it at home in that regard, would have suited me. It did not. An island here is nothing like the oasis. And while working at the harbor calls back some memories of the caravanserai, the repetitions made me restless again, too. After all the inability to just do as told got me into trouble in the first place.

The Empire taking tighter control of the island, the governor's man likely to eventually spot the mark, made it easy to finally break with aspiring to match the expectations others tried to slip over me. No rest for the wicked!

Corsair

Nothing is really for nothing. The Karelagne Crown's navy did do one thing for me though. They made me a battle-hardened sea soldier. And that served me pretty well with my first crew, first time pirate -- deckhand and fighter boarding. The simple hard work and the sharp edge. That transition was easy.

I should however have picked the crew more carefully. Turns out pirates can live up to the expectations quite well, the whole brutality of plundering and such. The captain was nothing like mine in the navy. But while he did treat the crew well, nothing to complain there at all, we were quite a blood-lusty bunch when it came to the piracy business.

Code

One thing you learn quickly once you trade imperial chains for a free deck is that throwing off the law is the easy part; deciding what replaces it is where crews either hold together or rot from the inside. Back in Karelagne, the Crown and the Church love to paint every corsair and privateer with the same brush—godless cutthroats who would slit a throat for a copper button. And truth be told, without some kind of leash, a ship full of armed outlaws can slide into that exact sort of ugliness fast. If you throw out the lords’ laws and the theologians’ commandments only to become the same kind of monster as that sadist first mate I fled—just with worse manners and no uniform—then what was the point of flying away at all?

That is why the proper crews—the real corsairs and free-sailors worth their salt—swear by a Code. Nothing sacred, nothing carved into stone tablets by smug inquisitors, just hard-won agreements written in rum and ink: you split the take fairly, you do not prey on those who have nothing, you stand by the mate bleeding next to you (unless they fall behind, of course, though a decent crew at least thinks twice before leaving you on a sandbar), and you never, ever sell another soul into chains.

Naturally, spend enough evenings in a harbor tavern and you realize that half the captains in the Expanse treat the whole thing as more what you’d call guidelines than actual rules, especially once aetherium is on the table or someone invokes the right to parley. Still, having the words on paper matters. Even if plenty of sailors bend them whenever the wind shifts, a Code draws a line in the sand—and it tells you right quick which ships are worth boarding as a mate, and which ones deserve a broadside.

No Turning Back

Now, when I sit on a harbor wall watching the strange lights shimmer across the sky, the weight of it all catches up to me from time to time. There is no going home—not in twenty years, not ever. To Karelagne I am a deserter, a heretic, and a thief of Crown property, and in a realm this wild and unforgiving, an exile has no safety net if things go sideways. Between the storm-lashed void, the ancient things sleeping in the ruins, and two empires tearing the Expanse apart over every shard of aetherium, a single misstep out here does not just land you in chains; it erases you altogether.

Yet strangely enough, knowing the bridges behind me are burned to ash feels more like a blessing than a curse. Back home, my life was already written for me before I ever drew a breath—obey, endure, and break your back so lords and inquisitors could grow fat. Out here, the Expanse might kill me tomorrow, sure, but every step I take is mine. I have Unnammon’s wind in my wings, her blessed hatchet on my hip, and enough hard-earned sense to tell a real crew from a sinking ship. So let the world be dangerous. I am done waiting on the shore—I just need the right captain, the right mates, and a horizon worth chasing.

Available for Pirateering

All is well though. I'll now pick my chances more consciously, and will find a crew that lives and dies by a proper code. Maybe there is community out there, not asking for a family or anything. A friend or two would be nice. And a cook from Ayris would be...
